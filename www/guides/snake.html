Snake Strategy: Surviving a Long Tail

Snake is one of the oldest video game ideas still played today. You steer a snake that grows by one segment each time it eats, and the game ends if you hit a wall or your own tail. Early on it is easy. Later, your own body becomes the obstacle, so the game is really about planning space.

Use the whole board

Beginners dart straight toward the food. Better players follow a pattern that covers the board without crossing themselves. One of the simplest is to run along the outer wall in a loop and fill the middle in rows, like mowing a lawn. A fixed route means you always know where your tail will be.

Turn before the food, not after

If you steer sharply at the last second, you can trap yourself against your tail. Line up with the food a few squares early so your final move is a gentle one.

Never enter a pocket

A pocket is a closed area that your own body has formed. If the opening is smaller than your body length, you will not get out. Check the exits before you turn into any narrow corridor.

Control your speed

  • Take a breath on straight sections.
  • Press the next direction only when you are sure of the move.
  • On mobile, make clear, short swipes.
  • If the game speeds up, rely more on your route and less on reaction.

Where Snake comes from

The idea goes back to the 1976 arcade game Blockade, where two players tried to trap each other with growing trails. Single-player Snake spread widely in the late 1990s when it came installed on mobile phones. Its popularity continues because the rules are simple and every mistake is clearly your own.

Common mistakes

  • Racing toward the food along the shortest path every time.
  • Turning into the middle of the board early and cutting it in half with your own body.
  • Looking only at the head instead of the whole tail.

A route that works

Pick a loop around the outer edge of the board, and every time you eat, step in one row. When you reach the middle, work back out in the same fashion. Because the path is predictable, you can always see where your tail will be several moves from now.

Practice plan

Play a game where you ignore the score and only try to follow the same route for five minutes. After that, add the food back in, and bend the route only when the food is within two squares.
