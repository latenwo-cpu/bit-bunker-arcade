About Dots & Boxes

Dots & Boxes is a pencil-and-paper classic turned digital: players take turns drawing a line between two adjacent dots, and completing the fourth side of a box claims it — and earns another turn.

How to play Dots & Boxes

Click between two adjacent dots to draw a line on that edge. If your line completes a box, that box is marked with your color and you get to move again. Most claimed boxes when the grid is full wins.

Tip: Avoid being the one to draw the third side of any box whenever possible — that's the move that hands your opponent a free box on their next turn.

Strategy guide

Avoid drawing the third side of any box, because it hands your opponent a free capture. Near the end, count the chains and loops to decide who is forced to open them.

A little history

The French mathematician Édouard Lucas described this game in 1889, and it has fascinated puzzle fans for over a century.

More tips for Dots & Boxes

  • Avoid drawing the third side of a box unless you have to, because it hands the box to your opponent.
  • As the board fills, count the chains of boxes. The player who is forced to open the first long chain usually loses.
  • In a long chain, take all but the last two boxes, then leave them with a double-cross move to keep control.
  • Early on, play safe moves that don't give anything away and make your opponent run out of them first.
