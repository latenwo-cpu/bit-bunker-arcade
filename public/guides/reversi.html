Reversi Strategy: Corners and Mobility

Reversi is played on an 8x8 board with discs that are black on one side and white on the other. You place a disc so that it traps a line of the opponent's discs between your new disc and another of yours, and the trapped discs flip to your color. When neither player can move, whoever has more discs wins. The surprise is that having the most discs in the middle of the game is usually bad.

Corners are everything

A disc in a corner can never be flipped, because it has no line passing through it from both sides. Corners also anchor stable edges that your opponent cannot flip. Try to take corners, and avoid the squares next to them, especially the diagonal square, because those usually hand the corner to your opponent.

Mobility beats disc count

Mobility means the number of legal moves you have. If you flip too many discs too early, your opponent gets many choices while you run out of safe moves. Aim for moves that flip few discs and keep your discs inside the group rather than on the outside.

Edges and stability

Discs on an edge are harder to flip than discs in the center, but a lone edge disc can be attacked. Try to build edges anchored by a corner. Stable discs, those that cannot be flipped for the rest of the game, are what you are really collecting.

Opening ideas

  • Play quiet moves that flip one or two discs.
  • Stay in the center and let your opponent use up their safe moves first.
  • Count legal moves for both sides before you play.
  • Do not grab discs for their own sake.

The endgame

In the last ten or so moves, count carefully. Forcing your opponent to pass, or taking the last safe move in a region, can swing the final score by many discs.

Common mistakes

  • Taking the move that flips the most discs every turn.
  • Playing next to an empty corner and giving it away.
  • Running out of safe moves while your opponent has plenty.

A worked example

Say you can choose between a move that flips six discs along an edge and a quiet move that flips one disc in the center. The six-disc move gives your opponent new lines to attack and may open a corner. The quiet move keeps your options open. Strong players choose the quiet move most of the time.

Practice plan

Play against the computer on Easy and count your legal moves before every turn. If you have fewer than your opponent, play to increase your options. Then move up a level and try to take all four corners.
