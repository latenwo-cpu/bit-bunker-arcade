Hangman Strategy: Which Letters to Guess First

Hangman is a word-guessing game where you reveal a hidden word one letter at a time, with a limited number of wrong guesses. Good players treat it as a small statistics problem rather than luck.

Start with common letters

In English, the most frequent letters are E, T, A, O, I, N, S, H and R. Vowels appear in nearly every word, so begin with E, A, O and I. They reveal the structure of the word quickly and let you eliminate possibilities.

Use the word length

Short words are different from long ones. A three- or four-letter word often contains just one vowel, while a ten-letter word almost certainly has several, and endings such as -ING, -ED, -ION and -ER become likely. Once you see a pattern, such as _ _ _ I N G, guess the letters that fit that pattern.

Think in patterns

  • Q is almost always followed by U.
  • Double letters are common in LL, SS, EE and OO.
  • If you find a T, try H next to it, and the other way round.
  • A word that starts with a vowel usually has few other vowels.

Avoid rare letters early

J, Q, X and Z are tempting, but they are rarely in the word, and every miss costs you a life. Save them for when the pattern clearly points to them.

Learn from every miss

A wrong guess is information too. If E is not in the word, you can rule out a large set of words, which narrows your next choice.

Why it is good for you

Hangman builds vocabulary and spelling and teaches kids to reason from partial information. It also works well as a pass-and-play game with friends.

Common mistakes

  • Guessing letters in alphabetical order.
  • Ignoring the pattern revealed by earlier guesses.
  • Picking a rare letter just because the word feels unusual.

A worked example

You see _ _ _ _ _ and the guesses E and A are wrong. You now know the word has no E or A, so it is likely to use O, I or U, and common consonants such as S, T, N and R. A good next guess is O or I, then T or R. Each wrong guess has already helped you remove a large group of possible words.

Practice plan

Play ten words and write down your opening letters. Keep the ones that worked and change the ones that did not. Playing with a friend who picks the words gives you a wider vocabulary to practice on.
